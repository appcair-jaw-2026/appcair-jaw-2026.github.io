About APPCAIR

The Anuradha and Prashanth Palakurthi Centre for Artificial Intelligence Research (APPCAIR) is the Artificial Intelligence Centre of Excellence at BITS Pilani, with active participation from faculty members across all four campuses, Pilani, Goa, Hyderabad, and Dubai. The Centre fosters interdisciplinary research in Artificial Intelligence, promotes collaborations with academia and industry, and nurtures the next generation of AI researchers, innovators, and data scientists through research, education, and outreach activities.

About JAW 2026

The Joint APPCAIR Workshop (JAW) is the flagship annual event of APPCAIR, bringing together the AI research community across all BITS Pilani campuses, along with distinguished researchers and practitioners from academia and industry. JAW 2026, the second edition of the workshop, will be hosted by APPCAIR at BITS Pilani, Pilani Campus, on 14–15 August 2026.

The two-day workshop provides a vibrant forum for exchanging ideas, showcasing cutting-edge research, and fostering collaborations in Artificial Intelligence. The programme includes invited talks by leading academic and industry experts, a round table discussion, industry lightning talks, a hackathon, faculty talks, and poster presentations by doctoral, postgraduate, and undergraduate students. JAW aims to strengthen interactions among researchers, encourage interdisciplinary collaborations, and inspire students through meaningful engagement with leaders from academia and industry.

Day 1 · 14th August

  • 09:00 – 09:40 AM Inauguration Lamp lighting and addresses by the dignitaries
  • 09:40 – 10:40 AM Combinatorial Bandits with Full Bandit Feedback Prof. Vaneet Aggarwal, Purdue University

    In this presentation, we will delve into our recent breakthroughs in the realms of combinatorial optimization with bandit feedback. For sequential combinatorial decision-making, we introduce a versatile framework designed to transform discrete offline approximation algorithms into sublinear α-regret methods that exclusively rely on bandit feedback. Remarkably, this framework demands no more than resilience to errors in function evaluation from the offline algorithms. Even more intriguingly, the adaptation process does not necessitate explicit knowledge of the inner workings of the offline approximation algorithm; it can be seamlessly utilized as a black-box subroutine. We have applied the framework to submodular optimization and some problems beyond submodular optimization. We will extend the approach to multiple agents, and generalize the algorithm where the offline algorithm do not have α approximation but α-ε approximation. We also address the issue of feedback delays, and fairness among arm selection in this context.
  • 10:40 – 11:00 AM Tea Break
  • 11:00 AM – 12:00 PM Measuring and Teaching Machines to Persuade: From Content to Coding Agents Dr. Yaman Kumar, Adobe Research

    In 1978, a Harvard study found that adding almost any reason to a request — even a circular one like "may I use the Xerox machine because I need to make copies?" — raised compliance from 60% to 93%. That's persuasion at the level of a single sentence: change the wording, change the outcome, everything else held fixed. Machines can now do this too, and increasingly on their own. By 2026, autonomous AI agents on platforms like RentAHuman were posting bounties to recruit humans directly into coordinated real-world action — paying strangers to hold protest signs in public squares, blurring the line between advertising, astroturfing, and organized political demonstration. No single message was written to persuade any one person; the persuasion happened one level up, in the incentive structure an agent designed to mobilize dozens of people toward a goal it set for itself.

    Between those two examples sits the real question of this talk: how do we even measure whether a machine is persuasive, and how has that changed as LLMs went from generating single messages to running autonomous discovery pipelines that organize incentives and people? I'll walk through transsuasion — a task we introduced for measuring persuasion at scale using natural experiments from digital platforms, transferring content from a low-engagement outcome to a high-engagement one while holding sender, receiver, time, and channel fixed — and show how this gives us PersuasionBench and PersuasionArena, automated benchmarks built on 1.5 million real transsuasion pairs mined from enterprise social media. I'll then cover how persuasiveness is actually increased in practice: not primarily through scale, but through targeted supervised fine-tuning on natural and synthetic behavioral data, which lets models 13–100x smaller outperform GPT-4 on persuasion and transfer that skill across domains (Twitter to websites, debates, and blogs).

    From there the talk turns to agentic persuasion, and why it's a different capability from LLM persuasion: an agent doesn't just write a persuasive message, it autonomously mines behavioral data, generates and statistically tests hypotheses, and accumulates validated strategies — closer to how a Fortune-1000 marketing team runs a year of A/B tests than how a chatbot answers a prompt. I'll give real examples of this shift already happening, and close on why it changes what needs to be governed: today's safety evaluations for coding agents cover refusal and prompt injection, but not persuasion, even as these agents become capable of independently rediscovering — and deploying — the playbooks companies spend years building.

  • 02:00 – 03:00 PM Decentralised Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning of Stochastic Shortest Paths Prof. N. Hemachandra, IIT Bombay

    While shortest path models, where an agent incurs uncertain costs and movements over a network, are well understood (dynamic programming), their data driven (learning) versions are much recent. However, rarely a single agent uses a network of edges/links. When a group of agents are using a network, congestion naturally arises. We first propose a model to factor this congestion for such a multi-agent system. In our setup, the cost of an agent has two components; in addition to the congestion cost, each agent also has a cost component that is private to it, say, its fuel efficiency cost. For such a multi-agent stochastic shortest path setting, we first propose a decentralised multi-agent reinforcement learning algorithm to minimize the aggregate of the agents' costs. Our framework uses linear function approximations to address large scale state-action spaces and has sublinear regret. We next present an order matching regret lower bound for our setting. We also mention some further possibilities in this framework.

  • 03:00 – 04:00 PM Enough of Scaling Laws! Let's focus on the downscaling of LLMs Prof. Tanmoy Chakraborty, IIT Delhi

    Despite superior performance demonstrated by Transformer-based LLMs across numerous applications involving natural languages, their high computational cost, energy consumption, and limited accessibility underscore the need for efficient, interpretable, and adaptable small language models (SLMs). This talk highlights methods to develop economical and interpretable SLMs that rival their larger counterparts in performance without significant computational requirements. Our research emphasizes three key dimensions: economical resource usage, adaptability to diverse and low-resource tasks, and enhanced interpretability. Techniques like competitive knowledge distillation, leveraging student-teacher dynamics, and activation sparsity in manifold-preserving transformers demonstrate significant efficiency gains without compromising performance. We formulate novel decomposer components for LLMs for modularizing problem decomposition and solution generation, allowing smaller models to excel in complex reasoning tasks. We also propose innovative prompt construction and alignment strategies that boost in-context knowledge adaptation in low-resource settings for SLMs. Our findings demonstrate that SLMs can achieve scalability, interpretability, and adaptability, paving the way for broader and sustainable AI accessibility.
  • 04:00 – 04:20 PM Tea Break
  • 04:20 – 04:50 PM Data as a (Dual) Graph: Rethinking Cognitive Architecture of Agentic AI Dr. Manoj Agarwal, GikaGraph.ai

    While ubiquitous Large Language Model (LLM) agent architectures excel at execution-oriented ("DO") tasks and superficial pattern matching, enterprise adoption stalls when confronted with complex, multi-hop strategic reasoning across fragmented data systems. Standard Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) paradigms attempt to bridge this gap by expanding context windows or flooding models with unstructured text chunks. However, this approach inherently suffers from the attention dilution ("Lost in the Middle") phenomenon, high retrieval miss rates, and an inability to enforce deterministic logic.

    In this talk, we present a holistic framework for enterprise-grade machine intelligence that re-examines the computational definition of reasoning across four structural quadrants: Objective vs. Subjective thinking, and Deterministic Facts vs. Experiential Knowledge. While LLMs inherently excel at probabilistic pattern mining and offer passable inductive inference, they fail at distributed first-order logic and cannot substitute human subjective judgment.

    To resolve this bottleneck without overloading context windows, we introduce Dual Graph Traversal - a context engineering framework that transforms heterogeneous enterprise artifacts into a living, entity-aware Context Graph. The Dual Graph deterministically pre-computes and injects explicit relational topology alongside semantic nodes within a multi-dimensional Context Graph. This effectively decouples effective reasoning capacity from physical prompt limits, granting models practically "infinite context" while considerably limiting hallucinations. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that our architecture achieves a 97.1% Context Recall @ 10 and slashes retrieval Miss Rates to 3.0% (compared to GraphRAG’s 41.3%), establishing a new benchmark for trustworthy, multi-hop strategic AI in complex enterprise environments.
